# Retrospective guide

**DDaT Ways of Working series**

---

## Why this exists

The retrospective is the named place where what is not working gets fixed. If it does not go here, it does not get fixed.

Most retros in council DDaT programmes fail not because the team has nothing to say, but because nothing changes after they say it. The session surfaces issues, the team vents or praises, a few ideas get mentioned, and by day three of the next sprint nobody remembers what was agreed. The same problems recur. The retro becomes a ritual without a function.

This guide fixes that at the source. The format is built around two things that the evidence says matter: a carry-forward check that holds the last retro accountable, and named improvement experiments that go straight onto the sprint board as stories.

---

## Format

The facilitator's job is not just to hold the structure -- it is to make honest engagement possible. Without safety, the format is a container that collects the answers people feel comfortable giving, not the answers the team needs.

**Duration:** 60 -- 75 minutes.

**When:** End of sprint, after the review. Before the next sprint planning meeting.

**Who runs it:** Delivery Lead or a rotating facilitator. Not the same person who ran the review.

**Who attends:** Full delivery team. Platform lead where cross-lane patterns are in scope.

---

### Part 1 -- Carry-forward check (10 minutes)

Before the team reflects on the sprint just ended, check the experiments from the last retro.

For each experiment:

- Did it happen?
- Did it work? How do we know?
- Keep it, drop it, or adapt it?

This is the discipline that separates a retro from a venting session. If last sprint's experiments are not reviewed, there is no feedback loop -- only a rolling list of good intentions.

*Watch: if experiments consistently did not happen, the retro is generating ideas the team does not have capacity or authority to act on. The next retro should address that directly.*

---

### Part 2 -- Sprint check-in (10 minutes)

A round-robin. Every person answers one question: **How did this sprint feel?**

One or two sentences each. No slides, no structure. The facilitator does not steer the answers.

This is not the data -- it is the signal. It tells the facilitator where the energy in the room is before the analysis begins.

The facilitator goes last. In a mixed team of contractors, secondees, and permanent staff, "most senior" is ambiguous and whoever answers first tends to set the frame for everyone else.

---

### Part 3 -- What worked, what did not (20 minutes)

Two columns. Write it on a board -- digital or physical.

**What worked** is not a celebration list. It is evidence of what to protect when the programme is under pressure. Name the specific thing, not the general feeling.

**What did not work** is not a complaints register. It is a diagnosis. For each item, the team asks: is this a one-off, or a pattern? If it is a pattern, what is causing it?

*Watch: if one column is much longer than the other, something is wrong -- either the team is not safe enough to name what worked, or they are not honest enough to name what did not.*

---

### Part 4 -- Generate experiments (15 minutes)

From the "did not work" column, the team identifies the two or three highest-priority patterns and generates one improvement experiment for each.

An experiment is not a task. It is a change to how the team works, with a named outcome and a way to know if it worked.

**Each experiment needs:**
- A clear change: what will be done differently?
- A named owner: one person, not "the team"
- A target date within the sprint: not "by end of sprint" -- a specific date
- What good looks like: one sentence that would tell the team the experiment landed

*Watch: more than three experiments per retro and the board fills with process work. Two focused experiments that close are worth more than six that drift.*

---

### Part 5 -- Improvement stories for the sprint board (10 minutes)

Each confirmed experiment becomes a story on the sprint board. Same format as any other story. One point. Named owner. Entered into ADO before the retro closes.

Retro experiments are first-class sprint work. If they are not on the board, they do not get done.

A simple format that worked well:

> **As a** [team / BA / Delivery Lead]
> **I want** [the specific change]
> **so that** [the outcome]
>
> **Done when:** [two or three observable conditions]

---

## The carry-forward principle

If an experiment carries from one sprint to the next, that is a signal -- not a failure. Name why it did not close. If it carries a second sprint without closing, it is not an experiment the team has capacity to run. Remove it from the board and decide: is it worth redesigning, or is it not the right change right now?

A retro improvement story that never closes is not harmless. It tells the team that this kind of change does not happen here.

---

## Rules

**Do:**
- Run the carry-forward check before anything else. Last retro's experiments are the first item.
- Write experiments as sprint board stories before the session closes.
- Name a concrete owner for each experiment -- not "everyone" or "the team."

**Do not:**
- Generate a long list and narrow it after the session. Narrow it in the room.
- Run a retro without the carry-forward check. That is how patterns persist.
- Let the review become the retro. They have different jobs -- keep them separate.

---

## Healthy signals

- The carry-forward check takes less than ten minutes because experiments were small enough to close.
- The "what worked" column has specific things in it, not just "the team."
- Improvement stories are on the board before planning starts.
- The team can name, at the next retro, whether each experiment changed anything.

**Watch:**
- A retro where everyone agrees. That is a safety problem, not a healthy team.
- Experiments that keep appearing across multiple retros without closing. That is a structural constraint the team does not have authority to fix -- it needs to go up.
- A "what worked" column that is empty, or a "did not work" column that is empty. Both are signals that the session is not being run safely.

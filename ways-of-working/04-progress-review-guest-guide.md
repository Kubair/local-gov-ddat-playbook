# Progress review -- guest guide

**DDaT Ways of Working series**

---

## You have been invited to a progress review

This is a short guide to help you get the most out of it. It takes about five minutes to read.

---

## What a progress review is

A fortnightly checkpoint where the digital, data and technology team walks through what has moved since the last review -- use cases progressed, work produced, decisions made.

It is not a management presentation. The team walks through actual work.

---

## How the session is structured

**Duration:** 30-60 minutes. Depends on how much has moved and how many directorates are presenting.

| Time | What happens |
|------|-------------|
| 0-5 min | Welcome -- a quick word on how the session runs, then straight into the work |
| 5-50 min | Directorate round -- each directorate takes about 5 minutes to surface its headlines and show real work |
| 50-60 min | Open floor -- cross-cutting questions, challenge and feedback. Not a formality. It shapes what we do next. |

---

## The directorate round

Each directorate surfaces the headlines: what has been delivered, what is inflight and close to delivery, what is blocked, and any show-and-ask items. We go in the same order every time, so you know when your area is up. The detail belongs in post-review one-to-one calls -- we keep the session tight.

---

## How to come prepared

**No homework.** You do not need to read anything beforehand or bring anything with you. Just show up. Curiosity is enough.

**Think about your service.** Some of what we show will land in your service. If you spot a connection -- good or concerning -- raise it when that directorate is up, or in the open floor.

**Speak freely.** We show real work, not polished presentations. Your questions are the test the work needs. There are no silly ones, only unasked ones.

---

## Your role

You are not here to be updated. You are here to engage.

If a use case involves your service area and something looks different from what you expected, say so. If a decision is presented that you have a view on, this is the time to share it -- not afterwards in an email.

The most useful thing a guest can do is ask a direct question about work they are not sure about.

---

## If you cannot attend

The review is not recorded. A short written summary is circulated after the session. If your service area is covered, the relevant section will include any decisions or actions that involve you.

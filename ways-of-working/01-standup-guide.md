# Stand-up guide

**DDaT Ways of Working series**

---

## The format

**Duration:** 10-15 minutes. Hard stop.

**Frequency:** Daily, same time each day.

**Who runs it:** The team. The facilitator holds the space -- they do not own the update.

**Rule:** Update your board card before the stand-up starts. The stand-up reflects the board -- it does not create it.

---

## Facilitator role rotates

A different person takes it each sprint. This is deliberate -- it builds shared ownership and stops the stand-up becoming a one-person show.

The facilitator:
- Starts and ends on time
- Moves the call forward
- Names blockers and agrees who picks them up, then parks them
- Does not answer on behalf of anyone else

**What this sounds like in practice:** When a blocker comes up mid-update, the facilitator says: "That is a blocker -- noted. [Name] and [Name] pick that up after this call. Keep going." The update continues. The blocker gets resolved offline, not during the stand-up.

---

## Five formats -- pick one per sprint, retro on it

### Three questions
**Best when:** work is clear and moving, tasks are well-defined.

1. What did I complete since the last stand-up?
2. What am I working on today?
3. Is anything in my way?

*Watch: vague answers mean work is not broken down enough.*

---

### Walk the board
**Best when:** the team is building card-ownership habits and the board is the source of truth.

Facilitator shares the board on screen. Walk right to left -- Done, In Progress, To Do. Each card owner gives: status, next step, any blocker. Unowned or stale cards get named and fixed after the call.

*Watch: a messy board shows immediately. Keep pace.*

---

### Use-case check-in
**Best when:** discovery or analysis is live and daily output is hard to pin down.

Each person names their active use case, where it is (discovery / analysis / ready / testing), what moved, and any handoff needed -- and who acts on it.

*Watch: updates should prompt action after the call, not during.*

---

### Commitment check-in
**Best when:** sprint planning commitments are specific enough to look back on.

1. What did I commit to in sprint planning?
2. Did it happen? If not -- one sentence why.
3. What does that mean for the sprint goal?
4. What am I committing to today?

*Watch: only works if the team feels safe naming what did not happen. The most senior person in the room answers first -- this sets the tone.*

---

### Blocker sweep
**Best when:** the sprint is slow, the same issues are recurring, or the team is stuck.

This format shifts the agenda from progress to unblocking. Use it when a standard update round would just confirm that nothing has moved.

1. What is your active blocker?
2. How long has it been in the way?
3. What have you already tried?
4. What does it need to move -- a decision, a person, a clarification?
5. Who owns the next step?

The facilitator names any blocker that has appeared more than twice. That is a decision that has not been made. Name it, agree who makes it, move on.

*Watch: if the blocker sweep runs every day, the sprint goal needs revisiting -- not just the blockers.*

---

## Rules

**Do:**
- Start on time. The call does not wait.
- Name blockers. Agree who picks them up. Move on.
- End early -- it is a good sign.

**Do not:**
- Solve things in the call. The stand-up surfaces problems; fixes happen offline.
- Report upwards. You are checking in with teammates, not giving a status update.
- Pad it out. Ten focused minutes beats twenty-five that drifts.

---

## Healthy signals

- Cards updated before the call. Nobody is surprised by the board.
- Blockers named without drama -- treated as normal, something to fix.
- The call ends early.

**Watch:**
- One person doing most of the talking -- a facilitation problem.
- The same blocker appearing more than twice -- it is a decision that has not been made.

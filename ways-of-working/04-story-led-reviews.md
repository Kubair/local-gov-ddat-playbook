# Story-led reviews

**DDaT Ways of Working series**

---

## What it is

A show and ask event -- not a status meeting.

The team presents done work to elicit feedback and spark collaboration. Guests are part of the conversation, not the audience. A list of completed tasks tells people what happened. A story tells them why it mattered, what got in the way, and where you are heading next.

---

## The format

**Duration:** 30-60 minutes. Depends on how much moved and the nature of what is being shown.

**Who runs it:** The Thread Lead carries the narrative. The team shows the work. Guests react and ask.

**The one rule:** Keep it informal. No one is seeking approval. Set that tone from the first minute.

---

## Seven steps

### 1 -- Set the scene *(Thread Lead)*
State the sprint goal and briefly narrate what the team pulled from the backlog to achieve it. One or two sentences. This anchors everything that follows.

### 2 -- Create visibility *(Thread Lead + Team)*
Walk through the sprint backlog -- what is done, what is not done. Be honest about what was carried forward and name the reason. Transparency here builds trust.

### 3 -- Share the journey *(Team)*
Briefly highlight what went well, what got in the way, and how the team worked around it. Keep it concise. This is the "how" behind the "what."

### 4 -- Show and ask *(Team)*
Show the done work -- live, not slides. Demonstrate the service, the data, the capability. Then ask guests for their reactions. This is the most important step. Silence is not acceptance. Keep asking.

### 5 -- The backlog as it stands *(Thread Lead + Guests)*
Show the board. Walk through what is sitting in the backlog. Invite guests to challenge priorities. This is where the next sprint gets shaped by the room.

### 6 -- What is next and why *(Everyone)*
The whole room collaborates on what to tackle next and why. The output feeds directly into sprint planning. Make priorities and decisions visible on the board.

### 7 -- Check out *(Everyone)*
One word or one sentence from each person. How did that land? What are you taking away? Closes the loop and signals that everyone's perspective was heard. If this step gets dropped because the session ran long, that is itself information -- it is a signal the session ran over, not a time-saving measure. Name it, and fix the timing next sprint.

---

## A note on rehearsal

If your team has not done this before, run a quick 10-minute rehearsal. Knowing your part makes the whole room more comfortable.
